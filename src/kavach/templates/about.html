What it does

Kavach checks a message you paste against a list of tricks that scammers use in India, taken from public advisories by RBI, NPCI, the Ministry of Home Affairs (I4C) and the Department of Telecommunications. It explains each warning sign in English, Tamil or Hindi and tells you what to do next.

What it does not do

  • It never says a message is safe. The best result is "No known scam pattern found".
  • It cannot check whether a phone number, UPI ID or link really belongs to a bank. It only spots impersonation patterns.
  • New scam scripts that avoid these patterns will not be caught.
  • It does not report anything for you. Call 1930 or use cybercrime.gov.in.

Your privacy

Kavach runs on this computer. It only accepts connections from this computer, does not send your message anywhere, does not save it, and does not keep logs of what you check. The page loads no outside fonts, scripts or trackers.

The wording check

Kavach also has a small statistical model trained on a public SMS phishing dataset. In testing it often gave high scores to ordinary bank alerts, because the normal messages in its training data are mostly personal chat. So it does not change the result; it is only shown in the details for English messages.
